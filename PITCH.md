# 3-minute demo script

**Setup before you start:** `.venv/bin/python app/server.py`, open http://localhost:8600 (front page). Keep three tabs ready: Flights at risk (ATL, 27 Jun 2025, 14:00), Capacity outlook (same), Performance.

| Time | Screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | **Front page**: let the approach scope run (amber = holding) | "This is for the Traffic Management Coordinator at Atlanta or Dallas. Today the arrival rate is cut once the storm is overhead, so aircraft circle at about 35 kg of fuel a minute. A Ground Delay Program keeps them at the gate instead, but it needs hours of warning. We give that warning 3 to 12 hours ahead." |
| 0:20–0:40 | Front page: "how a call is made" strip | "We join 4.6 million flights to the organisers' hourly weather plus public METAR, TAF and LAMP forecasts, all by script. A forecast made at time T only sees weather observed at or before T, and T is at least 3 hours before the flight; the code asserts it on every row. Wind is resolved onto the runway heading. Statistics make the numbers, fixed rules make the suggestions, the language model only reads forecast text, and the human decides." |
| 0:40–1:10 | **Flights at risk** (main target) | "Our main target: every flight's chance of a delay over 45 minutes. It's 2 pm on 27 June. Each flight has a calibrated risk and its top reasons: late inbound aircraft, a thunderstorm in the TAF, the route's recent record. On the 2025 test data it never saw: precision 31%, recall 54% six hours ahead, against 25% and 25% for the historical rate. One in three flags is right, so it tells you which flights to watch, not which to cancel." |
| 1:10–1:30 | **Try the model** → "Use 300 rows of the organisers' file" | "Judges can upload any CSV in the organisers' format. The same pipeline runs, and if delays are included it scores itself. Rows from before 2025 are scored but kept out of the metrics, because the model has seen those days." |
| 1:30–2:05 | **Capacity outlook** (bonus) for the same storm | "The bonus: hourly arrival capacity. At 2 pm it flags reduced capacity from 5 pm, shows the TAF it used with its issue time, and the rules suggest a GDP at about 51 an hour with the trigger shown. I accept; it's logged. Now 'Show what happened': this storm is also our worst miss. It flagged it in time but forecast 352 landings and only 169 happened, a ground stop. The convective warning tells the user not to trust the number. Toggle TAF outage: the numbers are withheld, not guessed." |
| 2:05–2:35 | **Performance** | "Tested on 2025: arrival-rate error 8.4 an hour against 9.2 for our schedule-only model and 11.8 for the TAF rule; 94% of NOAA-logged storms flagged 3 or more hours ahead. It works on Chicago without training on it. Weather's added value is modest overall but grows at 12 hours and in storms, where precision rises to 44%." |
| 2:35–3:00 | **Impact** | "Measured and estimated kept apart. With the cautious policy, replaying Jan–Jul 2025 moves about 670 hours of holding to the ground, about 1,400 tonnes of fuel, for 870 hours of extra gate delay. The aggressive policy saves more fuel but triples gate delay, so we recommend the cautious one. Thank you." |

**State the assumptions** (the organisers ask for this): label = DEP_DELAY/ARR_DELAY > 45 at ATL/DFW, cancelled and diverted flights (1.6%) excluded from the label; weather observed ≥ 3 h before the scheduled time; full BTS data for training (the provided file is a ~1% sample); headings ATL 80°, DFW 180°; impact is a queue-model estimate against a no-GDP baseline.
